# Capability parity audit: brainz vs gbrain

6 inventory lanes, 6 gap checks, 1 completeness critic, 1 report.
131 capabilities audited · 38 critical · 51 gaps · 58 partial

---

### Verdict

**No — not yet, and the gap is almost entirely in accuracy, not functionality.** On architecture, protocol shape, isolation and cost discipline, brainz is at parity or ahead: the substrate decision is sound, the seven-tool freeze is a defensible re-derivation of MEMORY_VERBS, and idea 5's "no request-time operation whose fan-out scales with corpus size" is a better-stated rule than gbrain arrived at by incident. But gbrain's answer quality does not come from `pgvector + tsvector + RRF`. It comes from a ~19-stage pipeline that runs *after* the candidate pool exists, and from a measurement apparatus that makes every stage falsifiable. Of those stages, the brainz design commits to **two** unambiguously (RRF fusion, token-budget packing), half-commits to four (vector arm, keyword arm, relational arm, intent classification), and does not mention thirteen. Separately and more fundamentally: **nothing in the nine cards causes the brain to be read or written at all.** `remember` is "the single create path" called by a caller nobody instructs; `recall` is a ranked read the host model has no directive to reach for. gbrain's two most load-bearing behaviors (signal-detector on every message, brain-before-answer on every question) live in a skills layer brainz deliberately deleted without replacing the mechanism — MCP's server `instructions` field, which the design never mentions.

**What is genuinely ahead:** physical isolation as *structure* rather than policy (a separate Neon project is stronger than gbrain's hardest-won `sourceScopeOpts` invariant, which is policy and has leaked twice); `origin_context` as an immutable credential-derived stamp, which is strictly stronger than gbrain's agent-typed `[Source: …]` line and its world/private flag; MCP annotation hygiene (`openWorldHint: false` on all nine, write-gating, endpoint visibility bound to the OAuth grant rather than a client header — gbrain sets none of this); idea 3's "nothing renders unless a user action is attached to it," which correctly kills gbrain's decorative `brain_score`; debt-triggered consolidation, better founded than gbrain's 300s autopilot poll; and export-as-the-self-host-format with a CI-gated round-trip, which is a stronger portability guarantee than gbrain ships. The honest summary: **the design is a match for what gbrain *is*. It is not yet a match for what gbrain *does at the moment of answering*, and it currently has no instrument that could tell you either way.**

### The accuracy stack — parity check

gbrain's `src/core/search/` is 32 modules. Roughly nineteen of them are quality-affecting stages between "query arrives" and "results returned." This is the ordered walk.

| Stage | What it contributes | brainz status | Risk if absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shared normalizer (NFKC + lowercase + whitespace) applied on write **and** read | The only thing keeping alias/title matching from drifting between index and query | **Absent** | Silent: alias table populated one way, queried another; no error, just misses |
| Zero-LLM intent classification | Routes the relational arm **and** sets `INTENT_WEIGHTS`, `effectiveRrfK`, exact-match boost, recency tilt | **Partial** — committed as a router only (idea 8); ranking role never stated | "who is Bob" and "what did I say last week" rank identically — most of what a consumer asks |
| Vector arm | Semantic recall | **Partial** — committed, but `input_type: query` vs document asymmetry unaddressed (both leading providers are asymmetric), and `hnsw.ef_search` defaults to 40 (H1) | Quietly degraded similarity on every query; presents as "our ranking is mediocre" |
| Keyword arm (tsvector) | Exact-term recall; one of three RRF arms | **Partial** — committed; FTS *language* never decided. `GBRAIN_FTS_LANGUAGE` configures the write-side trigger functions too, so it is a **provision-time schema decision per tenant** | Every non-English user runs one of three arms through an English stemmer, permanently, with no signal |
| Relational/graph arm | +31 P@5 in gbrain's benchmark (49.1 vs ~18 hybrid-no-graph) | **Partial** — arm committed (idea 8), **edge supply is not**. Typed edges come from LLM extraction, which idea 7 puts *below* the free-tier line | Free tier runs the single largest quality lever against an empty graph — the ~18 P@5 regime — while marketed at parity |
| Alias hop / exact-title / slug-suffix ladder | "The only layer that bridges true synonyms with zero surface overlap" | **Partial** — alias *data* committed (idea 9 authority file, idea 4 one entity); the read-path hop and the resolution precedence are not | Nicknames, maiden names, "the Acme deal" — the consumer case is heavier than gbrain's |
| RRF fusion (K=60) | Combines the arms | **Covered** — argued explicitly in the Vectorize rejection | — |
| Cross-encoder rerank (zerank-2) | **Reshuffles 60% of top-1 results** on a real corpus — the second-largest measured lever | **Absent** | The single biggest unclosed accuracy delta. Also fails open, so the objection is purely economic |
| Autocut (score discontinuity) | Result sizing | **Assumed ported, structurally broken** — reads `rerank_score` only, by design; gbrain measured RRF gap at 0.602 (right) vs 0.569 (wrong) and concluded it is mechanical decay, not signal | No-op at best; re-pointed at the RRF gap it cuts on noise — a precision regression with no error |
| Title-phrase boost | User types a thing's literal name and gets it | **Absent** | Maximally visible failure — and OpenAI's mandated `search` projection returns `{id, title, url}`, so title is *all the user sees* |
| Per-prefix recency decay (14–60d half-life on churn content) | Time-aware ranking | **Absent** | Dominant consumer queries are temporal; the committed connectors (mail, calendar, chat) are exactly the high-churn content |
| Source-type boost + hard-excludes (curated > bulk) | gbrain's **first** line of defense against contradictions, before any probe runs | **Absent** — idea 4 fences on origin but forbids origin a ranking role | A two-year-old newsletter outranks what the user stated last week; idea 7's model phases then adjudicate conflicts ranking should have settled for free |
| Graph adjacency + cross-source corroboration boosts | Independent attestation raises rank | **Absent** — and brainz is uniquely placed here: origin stamps make "calendar *and* mail both attest this" free | Corroboration is invisible; same-session chat content is not demoted |
| Compiled-truth boost (2.0× post-normalization on a canonical summary chunk) | The synthesis outranks the raw log | **Absent** — consolidation's output list (salience, entity cards, contradictions, commitments) contains no canonical summary chunk | "Who is Sarah" returns 200 raw emails instead of the current-state answer |
| 4-layer read-time dedup (top-3/page, 0.85 Jaccard, 60% page-type cap, 2 chunks/page) | Stops one verbose page monopolizing the answer | **Absent** — idea 7's "dedup" is corpus-level, a different mechanism | Mail threads and chat transcripts are near-duplicate-heavy by nature; worse here than in a curated wiki |
| Return policy (intent-adaptive sizing) | Trims the payload the *caller* pays for | **Absent** | Lower priority (off by default upstream), but the consumer economics are stronger |
| Token-budget packing | Bounds the payload | **Covered** — idea 8, plus idea 5's fan-out bound | — |
| Evidence + `create_safety` on the envelope | Tells the writing agent whether the thing already exists | **Absent** | `remember` is the single create path, driven by a model reading a bare score with no reason — every write is a duplicate-over-a-developed-page incident waiting |
| Contextual retrieval (embed-time `<context>{title}\n{synopsis}</context>` wrapping) | Anthropic's own measured technique; matters most on short fragments | **Absent** — and idea 9's empty state is *deliberately* short fragments | Highest-leverage missing write-side quality item; the title-only tier is free |

**Score: 2 of 19 committed, 4 partial, 13 absent.** Every absent row is invisible in production — no error, no exception, just answers that are quietly worse. That is precisely the H1 failure shape the design already knows how to reason about, replicated thirteen times.

### Critical gaps

**1. Nothing makes the agent use the brain.** In gbrain, signal-detector fires on every inbound message (capture) and brain-ops mandates consulting the brain before any external API or any answer about a person (retrieval). brainz deleted the skills layer without replacing the mechanism. The consequence compounds: no writes → no debt → no consolidation cycles → no salience, entity cards or contradictions → idea 5's live briefing assembles over an empty materialized layer, and idea 7's free/paid upgrade prompt ("14 contradictions") has nothing to count. A brain that is technically correct and never queried is indistinguishable from no brain while still incurring the consolidation bill. The available mechanism costs zero tool names: MCP's server `instructions` field plus tool descriptions plus idea 8's response envelope — a capture-and-consult directive the brain ships to whichever client connects. **The design never mentions server instructions.** → New card.

**2. The post-retrieval stack (see table).** Thirteen absent stages, two of which — reranking and the alias hop — are individually large enough to sink a parity claim. The unmade decision underneath most of them is a single one: **does `recall` get a rerank call and a query embedding, and who pays?** → New card.

**3. Nothing measures answer quality.** Idea 2 grades the *wire*: listTools, response schemas, error envelopes. It will pass green while write-back drops a fact, while recall misses something injected two sessions ago, or while a bad extraction prompt degrades every briefing every user reads. There is no owned corpus, no P@5/nDCG number, no absolute floor (gbrain ships nDCG@10 ≥ 0.65 as a hard bar), no NamedThingBench-class gates (title-substring Hit@1 ≥ 0.95, alias Hit@1 ≥ 0.98, dilution Hit@3 = 1.0), no extraction recall floor (gbrain's is 0.8, non-zero exit), and no grading of what consolidation *produced* — which is stored, not recomputed, so a bad prompt is permanent. Against the stated bar of "the same level of accuracy," this is the row that makes every other row unauditable in either direction. → New card, explicitly separated from idea 2 so the two are never conflated.

**4. The free tier is a structurally different product, and the design has not noticed.** The FREE TIER STOPS HERE line sits above extraction. Downstream: no typed edges → empty graph → relational arm dead; no entity cards → `entity` returns nothing; no salience → idea 5's briefing reads a null column and the whole bundle degrades to unranked; no extracted facts → long-form notes never become retrievable knowledge. And an unresolved contradiction lands here: **embeddings are inference.** If they are a model phase, free brains have no vectors and idea 7's "embedding-space clustering" free phase has nothing to cluster; if they are on the write path, the free tier calls a model. Idea 7 says truncation leaves a brain "consolidated but not dreamt, which is a state you can describe to a user" — that description has not been written, and what it actually describes is a brain that cannot answer "who is Sarah." → Idea 7: redraw the line, or ship a `graph_degraded`-class envelope flag with the same honesty as `search_degraded`.

**5. "Automatic ingest and background synthesis" is currently manual-by-proxy.** There is no ingestion substrate — idea 3's own downside concedes it. No recurring pull, no per-source cadence, no idempotent staggered retries, no per-item ingest log (which idea 3's "nothing from this source in 23 days" and idea 9's "a specific statement of what is not indexed yet" both *require*), no delivery channel. And conversation content — the thing gbrain's `synthesize` phase exists for — has no path in at all: an MCP server sees tool calls, not transcripts. gbrain's other path is the one brainz can actually use and hasn't designed: **bulk import of a user-uploaded chat export** (`src/core/conversation-parser/`). → New card.

**6. Authorization has no structural enforcement point.** Idea 8 states the problem and leaves it: *"the auth check has to live in shared dispatch below both handlers, and nothing structural enforces that."* brainz now has three surfaces (`/mcp`, `/openai`, ~20 `/admin` endpoints) with no single operation declaration behind them — the exact generation property that keeps gbrain's CLI and MCP from drifting. gbrain's cross-source leaks (#1924, #1371) came from precisely the inlined-per-handler pattern. Under idea 4 this is a cross-context leak; under idea 1 it is the isolation claim the whole product is sold on. Idea 4's own asymmetry says personal→work leakage "can end a career," and the two hard cases — graph traversal walking a work row to a personal row, and consolidation writing a synthesis over both halves — are named and unowned. There is exactly one CI assertion in this family (idea 3's `scope_denied`), and no zero-tolerance context-injection gate covering derived rows or post-severance reads. → Idea 8 + idea 4.

**7. The write path is undefined.** Idea 8 says "SQL plus a sub-cent embedding on write." Idea 7 says extraction is "a queue entry, not an inline step after a write." Nothing says where chunking, link extraction, and fact embedding sit — and gbrain's split is deliberate (facts embed *synchronously* so downstream classifiers have vectors immediately; chunks are backfillable; links are watermarked). Until brainz names its split, the two cost claims cannot be reconciled and every chunking capability is homeless. Concretely missing: a chunking spec at all; the hard 6000-char safety cap (two production bugs where over-limit chunks failed embedding *forever*, silently); and CJK-aware sizing (≥30% CJK triggers per-character counting — without it CJK text counts as one giant word, overflows the embedder, and the page imports but never becomes searchable). A consumer product going global inherits that failure on day one. → New card.

**8. Decisions that are irreversible after the first project is provisioned, plus no way to change them.** FTS language (trigger recreate + full backfill), a second embedding column for images (schema, not an argument — idea 8's additive-forever escape hatch future-proofs the *tool surface*, not the *storage layout*), embedding dimensions (pgvector HNSW caps at **2000d**, so several plausible defaults are unindexable), and taxonomy version. Against that: gbrain runs 120 ordered migrations against one database; brainz must run them across N, most suspended at $0, requiring a paid wake to touch, with partial-failure states the stateless fleet must tolerate at request time — and **no per-tenant feature flag exists**, so a new chunker or extractor prompt can only ship all-at-once across every isolated project. The substrate comparison rejected Multi-DB Schemas partly over migration fan-out; the fan-out did not disappear, it moved. → New card, sharing idea 7's warm-trigger and control-plane row.

**9. Model output ranks as truth, and can silently rewrite the user.** gbrain assumes an operator who promotes or rejects auto-extractions and demotes everything unverified until they do. brainz has no operator by construction — idea 3's action enum has no review verb, idea 9 deletes the CLI. So ~100% of derived knowledge is "unverified" in gbrain's sense, ranked as if the user typed it, with no `unverified` flag for the consuming model to weigh, no confidence gate on automated mutation (gbrain's is explicit: ≥0.8 auto-apply, 0.5–0.8 queue, <0.5 skip and log), and no quarantine on novel claims. Worse, idea 7 calls its phase contradiction *resolution*: silently overwriting a user's recorded opinion with a model's judgment is the most trust-destroying write a memory product can make, and gbrain's equivalent phase is deliberately **report-only**. Missing supporting mechanisms: prompt-injection sanitization in *and* out (Gmail bodies are attacker-authored text flowing into durable memory the user's agent reads back), and an anti-loop guard — idea 7's cycle reads the store it writes, so the self-extraction loop is structurally present and unguarded. → Idea 7 + idea 8 + idea 4 (the origin stamp should carry a trust level, not just a fence).

**10. There is no data lifecycle.** No lane of this audit owned it and the design has none: no page versioning or revert (gbrain: `get_versions`/`revert_version`), no soft-delete/restore/TTL trash/hard purge (72h tombstone, cascades chunks/links/relations), no blast-radius preview before a destructive op (gbrain's three layers: impact preview → typed confirmation → soft-delete with TTL), and no erasure path — `forget` is expire-not-delete and explicitly "distinct from account deletion," which is left undefined across Neon + R2 + control plane. Idea 4's "severance as a state transition" is a destructive operation with no preview and no undo. For a consumer product this is simultaneously the legal surface and the most trust-critical behavior in the product: **undo, after a nightly model phase rewrote something you told it.** → New card.

**11. First import is the largest cost event in the product's life and is ungated.** Idea 7's budget cap is debt-triggered and never sees the initial backfill. First Gmail connect is tens of thousands of items to embed in one burst, for a flat-priced user, on a 0.25 CU compute. gbrain's pre-flight gate is delta-aware, prompts interactively, and auto-defers to a capped background job in non-interactive mode. The consumer form of the prompt is a *product opportunity* ("importing 41,000 emails — start with the last 90 days?"), not just a guard. Related: nothing caps rolling per-tenant spend (the control-plane row already holds `tier`/`pending_debt` — add a rolling counter the scheduler already reads), and nothing hard-fails on an unpriced model, which is how an un-metered path shows up as a bill instead of an error. At $10–20/mo, COGS per user *is* the business model. → New card + idea 7.

### Important gaps

**Consolidation design (idea 7).** One brain-wide budget instead of per-phase caps means ordering is the only cost control — one expensive phase starves every later phase permanently and nothing reports it; add **importance-tiered selection** ahead of the model phases so truncation degrades by tier rather than randomly (the $50.71-vs-$0.96 incident was a *selection* failure — 1,985 items instead of 12, a 165× fan-out miss — which a budget cap does not fix). Salience is misplaced below the free line despite being arithmetic in gbrain (<60s on 50K pages). Clusters are computed but never materialized into pattern artifacts, so "what keeps coming up" has no read path. Derived facts are never invalidated when their source file changes — combined with never-delete `forget`, an edit that removes a claim leaves the old fact live and rankable forever. Link extraction is async, so "who did I just say Alice works with" answers wrong immediately after being told, and nothing says the link phase *reconciles* rather than accumulates. No per-cycle run record (phases, items, cost, truncation reason) for idea 3 to link to. No orphan detection — an entity with no inbound edges is unreachable by the relational arm regardless of its embedding. No **algorithm-version watermarking**: idea 2 promises "a number for how far behind you are," but a concept marked `implemented` only applies to content ingested after the change unless a `chunker_version`/`links_extracted_at` watermark forces a re-walk — and a brainz user never runs an upgrade, so the operator must push it. Idea 7's "staleness marking" already exists; widening it to algorithm staleness closes this in one sentence.

**Entity and graph modeling.** No edge-type vocabulary with declared inverses (a schema-pack concern brainz dropped) — an untyped edge degrades a relationship question to co-occurrence. No deterministic edge *sources* for connector content, though brainz's are richer than gbrain's and model-free (sender/recipient, attendees, thread participants). No entity-resolution ladder on `entity` — precedence, tie-break, per-arm degradation, and how the signed authority file loses to the user's own naming. Two alias primitives collapsed into one: slug redirects (required, or every merge/rename breaks idea 6's CI-gated export round-trip) and free-text synonyms. No typed claims (metric + value + validity window), so every number stays prose — "what rent/salary/dose was I paying and how has it moved" requires re-reading, the exact failure the product exists to remove — and no corroboration / forward-only supersession / quarantine on merge. No contradiction *verdict vocabulary* separating a real conflict from legitimate change over time, on a corpus where jobs, addresses and prices change constantly — and that count is already the paid-upgrade prompt, so false positives are a trust problem, not a quality one. Meeting back-fill needs the **body mention scan**, not just the attendee join — "who did they say they'd introduce me to" is the consumer value.

**Job substrate and fleet operations.** One queue with one job class, while the design already commits to three more (scheduled self-export, `search_degraded` backfill with inspectable per-job progress, and re-embed). Missing: typed job table, re-enqueue on worker death, stall detection, max-attempts dead-lettering, poison-job protection (a tenant that crashes the worker is permanently due under debt triggering), and lock-renewal-over-a-pooled-connection — gbrain lost ~39 worker processes/day to exactly that, and a per-tenant connection LRU reproduces it. No contention control: idea 7 answers pool contention with scheduling, but scheduling is not a mechanism — on a 0.25 CU compute the loser is the user's own query. gbrain's pace-mode lever is concurrency cap, not sleep. No fleet-side health rollup on the control-plane row: with one DB per tenant nobody opens 10,000 panels, so silent degradation is invisible until a user complains. Ticket shape signals completion, not progress or liveness — the difference between "still working" and "wedged" on a 41,000-email import is a support ticket.

**Protocol and surface.** Tool descriptions are treated as prose but are frozen into dated manifest epochs and may cost an enterprise re-scan to change; idea 8 names its own overlap (`recall(entity:)` vs `entity`) and concedes gbrain's +13–17pp routing result "was measured on markdown routing tables, not live tools/list selection." That needs a held-out intent fixture set with strict/lenient scoring, CI-gated — descriptions as a versioned, evaluated asset. Write-time near-duplicate dedup and supersession on `remember` is unspecified (idea 7's async dedup means recall can return two contradictory versions of one claim, budget-packed, in the window before a cycle). `entity`'s p99 promise is restated as warm-p99 + `cold_start`, weakening the one tool whose entire justification for a name is latency. Push-based context has a free home the design licensed and didn't use — volunteered pointers on the shared envelope — though the recent-turns argument needs a dated release. No protocol telemetry at all: per-verb counts, error rate, cold/warm latency split, budget-drop rate, entity-hit rate, measured time-to-first-stored-and-retrieved-memory (idea 9's own 60-second target has no instrument).

**Content sanity, multimodal, and embedding ops.** No ingest-time junk gate — a Gmail corpus is majority newsletter/receipt/notification boilerplate, and without gbrain's `content-sanity.ts` + quarantine (hidden vs warned-but-searchable) that content is embedded, paid for, ranked, and consolidated over; this is upstream of every dedup and ranking finding above. No policy for what `remember` does with a PDF, voice memo or photo — that happens in week one. No OCR (the dominant consumer image is a screenshot, which is mostly text; "find the screenshot with the wifi password" fails) — it is an LLM call, so it belongs in idea 7's model phase, not the write path. No raw payload preservation (`put_raw_data`): idea 6's file is the *rendered* page, not the connector's original, so re-deriving with a better extractor after a prompt improvement is impossible fleet-wide. No attachment/blob storage or signed-URL story. No storage tiering — if every ingested email becomes an exported file, idea 6's round-trip parity test runs over a corpus two orders of magnitude larger than conceived, and "export is the self-host format" means handing a user 40,000 machine-generated files. On embeddings: name the model and its measured basis (gbrain's answer — ZeroEntropy zembed-1 at 1280d, beat OpenAI 11/20 and Voyage 4/20, 442ms vs 973ms, $0.05 vs $0.13/M — is directly transferable and both latency and cost are load-bearing on cards brainz already committed), record the ≤2000d HNSW ceiling, and stamp a per-page provenance signature so a same-width swap re-embeds only affected pages. Also: no usage signal at all (`last_retrieved_at`), so brainz cannot distinguish dead content from merely old, and has nothing to prune by on a flat-priced storage bill.

**Product lifecycle as a user-visible thing.** gbrain's user *runs* the upgrade, gets a banner, gets a migration skill, and leaves a self-upgrade audit trail. A brainz user's memory changes behavior overnight with no notice, no changelog channel, and no consent. Add to that: no feature-discovery surface (in a product with no docs and no CLI, capability discovery *is* the growth surface), no friction/delight telemetry (a chat-panel product is unusually well-placed to capture it), no identity layer — `origin_context` answers "which credential," nothing answers "who is this person and what do they care about," which is what makes idea 5's briefing personal rather than a query result, and is the natural content of idea 9's one-act onboarding. And no cheap in-chat disambiguation primitive: the claim-URL handles consent but opening a tab is the wrong weight for "two people named Alex" — that is a response-envelope shape costing no tool name.

**Measurement plumbing.** Split the suite into a deterministic always-blocking half (seeded PRNG, zero LLM calls) and a sampled model-judged half on the canary — otherwise a noisy, model-priced gate gets marked non-blocking within a month, which is the exact death idea 2 predicts for its own ledger. Per-question-type floors, not aggregate (brainz's mix is unusually heterogeneous — relational, named-entity, temporal, context-fenced — so an aggregate is especially good at hiding one collapsed category that users experience as "it never remembers people"). Production `avg_rank1_score` drift is content-free telemetry and the only quality signal that works per-tenant across 10,000 databases. Give idea 1's canary tenant a second job: a nightly fixture run through the real `/mcp` on the real substrate, which also serves as the continuous post-deploy path check (extensions, GUCs — `ef_search` is per-connection and can evaporate — index, queue, tenant-string open). One correction for the porting-hazards card: the repo has **39** `check-*.sh` guards, not 42, and the ledger has no lane for hazards brainz discovers on its own substrate (H1's `SET LOCAL`-over-a-connection-LRU is a cross-*tenant* bug with no gbrain analog) and no card for the 6 privacy scanners, which matter more for a product holding real people's mail than they did for gbrain.

### Deliberately out of scope

| gbrain capability | Why brainz should not build it |
|---|---|
| Code indexing end-to-end — tree-sitter chunking (36 WASM grammars), call/import graph, `code_def/refs/callers/callees/blast/flow`, voyage-code-3 | No repository ingestion surface. gbrain carries it because it is a GStack mod. **Note: under idea 8's freeze these are permanent decisions, not deferrals** — six tool names could never be added without a manifest epoch. Say so in the card. |
| LLM multi-query expansion; LLM cross-modal tie-break | Per-query server-paid model call on the read path — the exact shape idea 8 cut `synthesize` to eliminate. Expansion's measured lift is barely above noise; the tie-break has no modality to disambiguate if images land as OCR-to-text. |
| Semantic and LLM topic-shift chunking as *user-visible modes* | Idea 9's one-right-answer is correct. But **pick the single chunker deliberately and record it** — the choice is quality-affecting even when only one ships. |
| The three-mode search picker | Right call. The knobs still need values; delete the picker, not the decision. |
| Multi-pack lenses; agent-authored schema evolution | Power-user ontology curation contradicts zero-setup. Consequence to record: taxonomy growth becomes brainz's centrally-owned release job, raising the stakes on the taxonomy card. **Calibration is a separate question and should not be dropped by association** — it needs prediction/outcome rows in the schema, so it is not free to add later. |
| Brain-resident skillpacks, the third-party quality rubric, endorsed/community tiering, the install nag | Defends a supply chain brainz does not have and should not create. Nothing is installable; there is no install path to attack. **Lift one property before dropping the rest: "the brain ships the instructions for using it, and they travel to whichever client connects" is exactly what critical gap #1 needs.** |
| The 52-skill markdown router; minion steerable subagents (pause/resume/replay); the CLI; gbrain's five-page operator SPA | Operator machinery. The host's own tool selector is the router; the durable job substrate underneath minion is *not* out of scope (see important gaps). |
| `brain_score` 0–100 composite | A number with no attached action — the sibling of the green dot idea 3 already rejects. Keep the sub-scores (embedding coverage, orphan rate, dead-link rate) as internal fleet telemetry. |
| Citation-rot repair; expert routing (`find_experts`); the generative surface (`brainstorm`/`lsd`) | Low consumer value or no input. The transferable pieces are the **confidence-gated mutation policy** from integrity-auto and the **selection-fan-out hazard** from the lsd incident — both belong on idea 7 regardless. |
| 21-provider embedding gateway breadth; per-provider column resolver | One hosted answer. The *asymmetric query/document* detail inside it is not optional. |
| Multi-source axis within one brain | Deliberately and well replaced by context. **One thing has no replacement: user-designated content excluded from ordinary recall** — a journal, therapy notes, medical records. `origin_context` structurally cannot supply it, being credential-derived and immutable. |

Every one of these should be an `omitted(reason, revisit_by)` row in `upstream/concepts.jsonl`, with `product:read-path-no-llm-spend` and `product:consumer-not-operator` as the two dominant reason codes. The absences that matter most are the ones nobody wrote down.

### What the design should add

**Existing cards that need to absorb something:**

- **Idea 2** — split explicitly into *wire conformance* and *product quality*; add a brainz-native hazard lane and the privacy-scanner cards; correct 42 → 39; widen from upstream drift to product instrumentation (per-verb latency split cold/warm, budget-drop rate, entity-hit rate, time-to-first-memory); a ledger entry is not `implemented` until existing brains have been re-walked.
- **Idea 3** — enumerate the consumer collector set (source staleness, unconfigured export, unresolved contradictions, low embedding coverage, review-queue depth); render the ranked list through idea 8's `brain` tool so it is model-reachable, not panel-only; add a one-tap confirm/reject.
- **Idea 4** — commit **one fence resolver** covering every read site, graph traversal and derived row, with a CI guard that fails on any handler-local auth check; add a zero-tolerance context-injection gate covering derived-row inheritance and post-severance reads; let origin carry a **trust level** and a **source-priority ladder**, not just a fence; add a **user-chosen exclusion facet** distinct from credential-derived origin.
- **Idea 5** — specify what a participant card contains; add a per-caller **read cursor** so the bundle carries a since-last-briefing delta (a snapshot with no delta is a dashboard, not a briefing); name a delivery channel or state plainly that briefing is pull-only.

### Open questions

1. **Where does the free/paid line actually sit — above or below extraction, and are embeddings free or paid?** This single answer decides whether the free tier is a product or a demo, whether the relational arm has edges, and whether idea 7's free clustering phase has anything to cluster.
2. **Does the read path get a rerank call and a query embedding, and who pays for them?** The largest single accuracy delta is an economic decision, not a technical one. Autocut is downstream of it and no-ops without it.
3. **Does `recall` get an optional budget/limit argument** — and if the answer is yes, does it ride a dated manifest epoch? The caller pays for payload tokens, which argues for the knob; the 8-param cut argues against.
4. **Is there any time-ranged read on the frozen surface?** `recall`'s params carry no date range and none of the seven tools is temporal. "What happened last Tuesday," "what changed since I last looked," "when did I last see Alice" are currently inexpressible **permanently**, not just in v1.
5. **Does `remember` return before or after embed, link extraction, and dedup?** Idea 8 and idea 7 currently imply different answers, and the cost claims cannot both be true.
6. **Which embedding model, at what dimension, and is it asymmetric?** Latency and cost feed idea 8's `entity` p99 and idea 7's economics; the ≤2000d HNSW ceiling rules out several candidates; asymmetric query/document handling is a silent-degradation bug if missed.
7. **Is FTS language a per-tenant provisioning decision, or is v1 English-only and stated as such?** It is a write-side trigger decision, so changing it later is a per-tenant backfill in an undesigned fleet-migration path.
8. **Does consolidation ever rewrite content the user stated directly?** "Contradiction resolution" vs gbrain's report-only drift phase is a one-word difference with the largest trust consequence in the document.
9. **Does the export corpus include machine-ingested bulk?** It determines whether idea 6's CI-gated round-trip runs over hundreds of files or forty thousand, and whether storage tiering is needed.
10. **What is the erasure story across Neon, R2 and the control plane, and does `forget` ever become a hard delete?** This is a legal question with a schema answer.
