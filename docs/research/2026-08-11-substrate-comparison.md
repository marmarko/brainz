# Substrate comparison: Turso vs Neon vs Vectorize vs DO brute-force

Workflow: 3 research lanes, 12 independently verified load-bearing claims (3 corrections applied), 1 synthesis.
Claims checked: 12 · corrections: 3

---

### Verdict

Build on **Neon, one project per user** — not one database per user, not Vectorize. The fact that decides it: a suspended Neon project costs **$0 compute and $0.105/month in storage** (0.3 GB × $0.35/GB-mo) while remaining a separately-addressable Postgres with its own connection string, compute endpoint, storage branch and role set — so "isolation the user can verify" and "near-zero idle" stop being in tension, which is the trade every other candidate forces. The incumbent loses on a narrower fact: Vectorize's documented topK ceiling is **100** (**50** the moment you set `returnValues: true` or `returnMetadata: 'all'`), while the ported stack asks the vector arm for `offset + max(limit*5, 100)` = **100–250 candidates**, and Vectorize has **no BM25 arm at all** — zero mentions of BM25, sparse vectors, or hybrid search anywhere in its doc bundle. It cannot serve the pool, and it cannot serve two of the three recall arms. Everything else here is cost arithmetic, and the arithmetic says the substrate is a rounding error next to inference: $0.41/user/month blended at 10k users, against downstream model spend that gbrain's own cost matrix puts at $40–1,000/user/month depending on search mode.

**Assumptions used throughout** (stated once, applied everywhere): typical brain = 2,000 pages × 4 chunks = **8,000 vectors** at 1,536-dim float32 = **49 MB raw**; total per-user Postgres footprint including HNSW (~1.7× on the vector column), text, tsvector, entities, edges, page metadata ≈ **300 MB** *[inferred, not sourced]*; usage = 30 MCP sessions/month × ~10 queries + a nightly consolidation ≈ **3.25 CU-hr/month** at Neon's 0.25 CU floor with a 1-minute suspend delay; **30% MAU** (per the brief: most brains idle in any month).

**Corrections applied** (where the digest and the verifier disagreed, I took the verifier):

- The digest's claim that Turso deprecated scale-to-zero for new signups and made new databases always-on is **wrong** — no authoritative page says that. The actual January 2025 announcement was a plan to migrate free-tier databases from Fly.io to AWS (eliminating cold starts), AWS still beta at the time, existing paid customers explicitly unaffected. I therefore treat Turso's idle economics as **unresolved**, not disproven — which is bad enough given the rest.
- "Turso Database is in beta" is **stale**: as of v0.7.0 (2026-07-13) Turso officially dropped the beta label. It is pre-1.0, and independent backups until 1.0 are still recommended per the GitHub README. This doesn't change the verdict; the ANN gap does.
- The digest's Workers guidance is **wrong in its specifics**: Cloudflare's own docs mandate `@libsql/client/web` and explicitly warn that "the non-web import will not work in the Workers environment" — the opposite of the digest's `@libsql/client/http` recommendation. The underlying thesis (no persistent filesystem, so no embedded replicas) is independently true.
- The brief's own premise that Vectorize drops to topK 50 "with metadata filtering" is **overturned by the research**: the documented triggers are `returnValues: true` and `returnMetadata: 'all'` — a bare `namespace` or `filter` does not reduce topK. I trust this (verbatim quote, docs dated 2026-04-21). It makes Vectorize *better* than the brief assumed and still not good enough.
- The 43× DiskANN bloat figure is a **single anecdotal non-maintainer bug report**, closed, no maintainer confirmation — the arithmetic checks (30k × 1024 × 4 B ≈ 117 MiB; 5 GiB / 117 MiB ≈ 43.7×) but it is not an official benchmark. I use it as an unspiked risk, not a settled number.
- The carried-forward "~29,000 pages" DO figure is the **768-dim** case (87,000/3). At the reference 1,536-dim it is 43,000/3 ≈ **14,300 pages**, and at 4 chunks/page ≈ 10,750.

### Where each one breaks

**Vectorize (index-per-user)** breaks at user #1, not at scale. The retrieval stack's vector arm asks for 100–250 candidates because RRF, per-page collapse and reranking all operate on the pool; Vectorize returns at most 100, and at most 50 once you ask for values back (which you need for exact rescoring or vector-space dedup). There is no batch-query API — one vector per `query()` — so reassembling a 250-candidate pool means N parallel calls, each separately billed, unioned client-side. And Vectorize ships no BM25, so two of your three recall arms live in D1 or DO SQLite while the third lives in Vectorize, with no transaction spanning them: a page ingested but not yet indexed is visible to the FTS arm and invisible to the vector arm, and RRF silently ranks a partial universe. The account cap of 50,000 indexes is a real wall at 50k users, but you'd have abandoned the design at 500.

**Durable-Object brute force** breaks on brain size, not user count, and there is no tier to buy your way out of it. Worker heap is 128 MB on Free and Paid alike; budgeting 64 MB for vectors holds ~43,000 int8 1536-dim vectors ≈ **14,300 pages at 3 chunks/page**. The first genuine power user — someone with a decade of notes — exceeds it, and your only lever is dropping to 768 dimensions or sharding one user's brain across DOs, which reintroduces fan-out fusion. Second problem: stateless MCP against a mostly-idle brain means most queries hit a cold isolate, so you pay a 64 MB load from DO SQLite into heap before the first dot product. Third: you are reimplementing HNSW-equivalent recall, BM25, RRF, graph traversal, dedup, recency decay and autocut in TypeScript, against a reference implementation that already exists in SQL.

**Turso** breaks on a bet, not a limit. Its per-tenant story is genuinely the best-fitted of the five — real files, unlimited databases on every paid plan since 2026-05-03, a Platform API built for signup-path provisioning, a <50 ms creation design goal. But the engine that has GA DiskANN ANN indexing (libSQL) is the one the company is walking away from, and the engine it is walking toward supports **exact/brute-force vector search only**, with ANN indexing listed as roadmap. If the 43× index-bloat report generalizes, a 32 MB corpus becomes a 1.4 GB index — **14 TB and ~$10,500/month at 10k users** — and DiskANN is documented as mutually exclusive with `BEGIN CONCURRENT`, so you choose ANN-with-serialized-ingest or concurrent-writes-with-exact-scan. Layer on Multi-DB Schemas and ATTACH being deprecated for new users (you hand-roll a migration runner iterating the Platform API across 10,000 databases), a Tantivy BM25 engine behind an experimental flag, and a company at ~$33M raised with no Series B, ~19–22 headcount by third-party trackers, and self-disclosed layoffs mid-rewrite. Any one of these is survivable. All five, on a substrate you cannot cheaply leave, is not.

**Neon project-per-tenant** breaks between 1,000 and 30,000 users as a *self-serve* option: Scale includes 1,000 projects, extra capacity comes in 500-project blocks at $50/month or by request, and the gated Agent plan starts at 30,000 projects per org. 100,000 is an enterprise contract with a company Databricks acquired for $1B — a stability signal, but also a pricing-power one. At 100k users and 30% MAU the run-rate is **~$32,250/month** in compute + storage plus project fees (~$42,000/month if you had to buy 99,000 project slots at $50/500), which against $10/user/month revenue is 4% COGS. The risk at 100k is contractual availability, not price. The other break is operational and self-inflicted: a naive "consolidate everyone at 03:00" cron wakes every project simultaneously, converts your $0.105 idle users into $0.83 active users, and hammers the Neon API.

**Neon database-per-tenant** is disqualified twice over. The hard cap is **500 databases per branch on every plan**, so it breaks at 500 users. Worse, it never isolated anything: a branch has a single primary read/write compute endpoint and every database inside it shares that compute, so one tenant's re-embed contends with every other tenant's query, and scale-to-zero is impossible while any tenant in the project is active. It is a `tenant_id` column with better ergonomics.

### The Neon question, answered directly

**Database-per-account limits.** The cap that binds is **projects per account**, not databases. Free: 100. Launch: 100. Scale: **1,000**, extendable in 500-project blocks at $50/month, and explicitly "can be increased on request." The gated Agent plan starts at **30,000 projects per org** by default, raisable, and requires an active Scale plan plus a card. Databases-per-branch and roles-per-branch are each **500, hard on every plan including Enterprise** — which matters not at all if the tenant unit is a project holding exactly one database and one role. So: 1,000 users is inside the Scale default; 10,000 costs 18 blocks at $900/month or an Agent-plan conversation; 100,000 is a negotiation.

**Is a free-tier-sized database enough per user?** As a resource envelope, comfortably yes. Free per project is 0.5 GB storage, 100 CU-hr/month, a 2 CU autoscale ceiling, 10 branches, and a fixed 5-minute suspend delay. A 2,000-page brain is 8,000 vectors × 1,536 dims × 4 B = **49 MB of raw vectors**; HNSW adds roughly 1.5–2× on that column; add chunk text, tsvector, entities, typed edges and page metadata and you land near **150–300 MB** *[inferred, not sourced]*. That fits 0.5 GB with room for a 4,000–6,000-page brain. The tighter constraint is not storage but the **0.25 CU / 1 GB RAM floor during a full re-embed and HNSW build** — that works but is slow, so budget the 2 CU burst for consolidation. The thing you cannot do is give each signup their own free Neon account: Neon's AUP discourages multiple accounts to multiply free-plan resources *(intent confirmed; exact clause text not retrievable)*, which makes it a ToS problem rather than a capacity one. Read the free tier as evidence that the envelope is right-sized, then pay for it on one Scale or Agent account.

**Does Hyperdrive disqualify per-tenant Postgres from Workers?** It disqualifies the **Hyperdrive path**, categorically, and it disqualifies nothing else. A Hyperdrive config is pinned to exactly one connection string, and the account cap is **25 configs on Paid, 10 on Free**, each capped at ~100 (Paid) / ~20 (Free) origin connections. Ten thousand tenants would need ten thousand configs against a ceiling of 25 — the wall is at tenant #26, not tenant #10,000. Two clean exits, and neither is a compromise. First: `@neondatabase/serverless` talks HTTP/WebSocket through Neon's own edge proxy, holds no Hyperdrive slot, and is named by both Neon's and Cloudflare's docs as the documented fallback "when Hyperdrive is not available for your setup" — which is precisely this shape. Second, and better here: **don't run the MCP server on Workers.** Nothing about stateless MCP requires an edge runtime; a stateless Node/Bun fleet gets pooled TCP, prepared statements, `postgres.js`, a per-tenant connection LRU, and full pgvector semantics, and each Neon compute's own PgBouncer accepts `max_client_conn=10000` **per endpoint**, so there is no account-wide connection ceiling to hit. The Hyperdrive limit is real and it is the likeliest hard blocker only if you assume Workers; drop that assumption and it evaporates.

### What I would actually build

**One substrate. Neon Scale, one project per user, one branch, one database, one role.** The connection string is the isolation receipt: hand a skeptical user their own `postgres://`, let them run `\l`, and the promise is structurally verified in one command. That is not available from any other option on this list.

1. **Warm pool of pre-provisioned empty projects.** Keep 50–200 empty, migrated projects idle; signup assigns one and stamps the user ID instead of calling create-and-wait. A cron refills the pool. This converts the one genuinely unbounded number in the whole design — Neon publishes no SLA for create-project → create-role → create-database → first successful query — into a constant-time pointer swap, and satisfies "database exists seconds later" with margin. Idle pool cost is 200 × $0.105 = **$21/month**.
2. **Suspend delay = 1 minute** (a Scale-plan-only setting; Launch is fixed at 5). Idle user = **$0.105/month**, all storage. Accept the ~500–870 ms first-connection wake (Neon's own end-to-end benchmark; the "few hundred ms" figure excludes TLS and geography) — with a 1-minute delay you pay it once per MCP session, not once per tool call.
3. **MCP server on a stateless Node/Bun fleet, not Workers.** Per-tenant `postgres.js` connection LRU capped around 500 warm connections, evicted by recency. Skip Hyperdrive entirely. Keep `@neondatabase/serverless` in reserve for anything that genuinely must live at the edge.
4. **Port gbrain's schema and query layer unchanged** — it is already Postgres + pgvector, which is the entire point of choosing this. Create the HNSW index from day one and **`SET hnsw.ef_search = 250` per query**. The default is 40; leave it there and your 250-candidate pool silently returns 40 candidates, RRF fuses a truncated universe, and autocut fires on a distribution that doesn't exist. This is the single most likely production bug in the port and it is one `SET` away.
5. **Markdown in R2**, keyed `{user_id}/{path}.md`, $0.015/GB-month with zero egress. 10k users × 50 MB = 500 GB = **$7.50/month**. Postgres holds metadata, chunks, embeddings and typed edges; R2 holds bytes.
6. **Stagger background consolidation by user-ID hash across the night**, bounded to ~20 concurrent tenants. This is not a nicety: consolidation is the only thing that touches every project, and running it as a thundering herd is what turns a $4,125/month bill into a $9,200/month bill and trips Neon's API limits at the same time.
7. **Do not build a vector-layer abstraction.** The evidence settles this and the abstraction is not free — it would have to expose the intersection of the candidates' capabilities, which is Vectorize's capabilities (topK ≤ 100, no joins, no BM25, no recursive CTEs, 1,536 dims), and that intersection cannot run the product. You would be paying an ongoing tax to preserve optionality on a door that is already closed. **The one thing to keep loose is embedding dimensionality**: put the column name and dimension behind config, because 1,024-dim Voyage vs 1,536-dim OpenAI is a live choice and pgvector's HNSW ceiling (2,000 for `vector`, 4,000 for `halfvec`) accommodates either — that is a config key, not an architecture.

**The tier that is worth building is inside Postgres, not across vendors:** exact scan versus HNSW. At 8,000 vectors a 0.25 CU compute scans 49 MB in roughly **80–150 ms** with 100% recall and arbitrary filtering. At 40,000 vectors (10,000 pages) that becomes 245 MB and **250–500 ms**, which is too slow. So the boundary sits near **20,000 chunks (~5,000 pages)**, and the measurement that sets it is p95 of the vector arm alone, measured at the tenant's actual compute size — not a synthetic benchmark on a bigger box. In practice, build the HNSW index unconditionally (the planner will seqscan when that's cheaper) and treat the threshold as the point where you stop worrying about index build cost during ingest.

**The decision worth deferring, cheaply:** `halfvec`. It halves the vector column and its HNSW index (49 MB → 24.5 MB per brain, ~1.5 TB → ~750 GB at 10k users, saving roughly **$260/month**) at a small, measurable recall cost. It is an `ALTER TABLE ... USING` away, changes no query text, and nothing you build now forecloses it. Defer it until storage is actually a line item you care about. The same is true of sharding power users' brains — nothing in the schema forces that call today.

### Open questions

Three, and only the first one can change the answer.

1. **Neon's real ceiling and price above 30,000 projects, in writing, before you sell a 100k-user roadmap.** The Agent plan's 30,000/org default is "raisable" and Scale's block pricing ($50 per 500 projects) may or may not extend linearly to 200 blocks. If Neon won't commit past ~30k on non-punitive terms, the 100k plan is a different architecture — self-hosted Postgres-per-tenant on CloudNativePG, which costs you the scale-to-zero that made idle cheap and puts you back in the always-warm world you were trying to escape. This is a commercial question with an architectural answer attached, so ask it early and cheaply.
2. **Measured p50/p99 for create-project → create-role → create-database → first successful query.** Neon publishes no number. Provision 100 projects and time it; it costs an afternoon. If p99 exceeds ~3 seconds, the warm pool goes from a nice optimization to load-bearing — which is why I'd build it regardless and treat this measurement as sizing the pool, not deciding whether to have one.
3. **Whether FTS5 is compiled into Durable-Object SQLite.** This only matters if question 1 goes badly and you need the DO path as a fallback for a cost-constrained free tier. FTS5 is a compile-time module rather than a loadable extension, so the brief's "no loadable extensions" constraint doesn't settle it either way.

Not open, and I would not spend a spike on them: Vectorize's topK semantics (documented verbatim, and irrelevant once you accept it has no BM25 arm), Turso's engine discontinuity (confirmed against the vendor's own repo and roadmap post), the Hyperdrive 25-config ceiling (confirmed, and routed around rather than solved), and the DiskANN bloat report (unconfirmed as a general fact, but Turso is already out on the ANN-transition risk alone, so settling it changes nothing).
